Case 01 · 2026

X.Global — Website Redesign

UX/UI Design · Product Thinking · Web Design

01. About the project

Redesign of the multi-page X.Global online-school website that I originally built on Tilda two years earlier. The task was to rethink the user experience while preserving the school’s visual identity and developing a modern, responsive interface.

The goal: an easy, quick path to a trial lesson and fast access to the information people need to make a decision.

My role: UX/UI Designer, Product Designer, art direction, interface design and development with AI.

1 / 1

02. The problem

The original website primarily provided information. Visitors had to find details about teachers, learning formats, outcomes and registration by themselves.

Core UX problem: no clear journey from discovering the school to booking a trial lesson.

UI problem: weak responsiveness and inconsistent interface patterns.

1 / 1

03. The goal

Discover → Choose a direction → Build trust → Try a lesson

Reduce the steps required to submit an enquiry, make the school’s offer easier to understand and create a comfortable mobile experience.

04. Key decisions

Information architecture

Preserved the multi-page structure and redesigned the hierarchy of information.

Product thinking

Four entry scenarios: exam preparation, improving school performance, university subjects and languages.

Conversion UX

A floating CTA available on every page and a short booking form in a modal window.

1 / 1

Trust design

Teacher profiles, student results, real reviews, case studies and school documents.

UI system

Retained the brand palette and handwritten typography; developed a card system with soft neumorphism.

Responsive design

Responsive grids, mobile navigation, horizontal galleries and touch interactions.

Localization

Russian and English versions of the interface.

Teachers, trust and the school’s offer

05. Implementation

A multi-page website built in HTML, CSS and JavaScript with AI-assisted development.

Tools: Figma, ChatGPT, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, GitHub, Netlify.

1 / 1

06. Outcome

Designed and deployed a responsive X.Global website with a consistent visual language, a structured offer and a clear user journey.

The key result: moving from an informational website to an interface that supports decision-making and booking a trial lesson. SEO metadata, media optimisation and accessibility states are included.

The project is being iterated. Conversion results have not been measured yet. Analytics events are built in; planned metrics include Task Success Rate, Time on Task, Drop-off Rate and Conversion.

Polosa — music by independent artists

The first CTA: start listening

The player’s Play control is the first primary action: hear a track directly on the home page before deciding to explore the artist. Search supports visitors who already have a specific artist or release in mind.

The intended next steps are exploring the artist, saving a track and returning. This is the interface hypothesis; its impact has not been measured yet.

1 / 1
Problem context
01

Niche music struggles to compete for attention

On major streaming platforms, users often remain inside familiar algorithmic feeds, while discovering smaller artists takes extra effort.

02

Artist support is fragmented across platforms

Listening, artist information, music purchases, and merch often live in separate services.

03

Information overload keeps growing

In a constant stream of content, it becomes harder for users to notice new artists they might genuinely like.

04

Payment can be difficult for part of the audience

For Russian users, purchases on international platforms often require additional steps.

Listening, artist updates and purchases are often split between services. Polosa brings these steps into one experience.

Product goal

Shorten the journey from discovering an artist to saving them, returning, and choosing to support them.

Player and artist screens

Research

How people discover new music

A survey of active independent-music listeners in Saint Petersburg revealed recurring patterns in discovery, saving, and purchasing behavior.

Journey map insights

Three critical moments

Discovery

“Who is this, and should I keep listening?”

Music, brief context, and current events are brought together in one place.

Return

“How do I avoid losing track of this artist?”

Following and updates help the relationship continue after the first interaction.

Purchase

“Why is this worth my money?”

The price is supported by product details, uniqueness, and context from the artist.

Research findings & user flow
01

No exact query

Discovery often starts with recommendations and browsing rather than a search field.

02

Music comes first

An artist’s first screen should let users hear the music as quickly as possible.

03

Saving is the core action

“Save” and “Follow” are the key actions after first discovery.

04

Return loops need to be designed

After following an artist, the product should create reasons to return: a release, concert, or artist update.

05

Purchase comes later

The store should not compete with the first listening experience. Interest comes first.

06

People buy value

Users buy a desirable object and a closer connection to the artist, not only the idea of providing support.

Case 03 · Discovery / Research

Supported Access

Inclusive Digital Services · Product Design · UX Research · Social Impact

Role: Product Designer, UX Researcher · Type: social impact / competition project

About the project

Supported Access is a concept for an inclusive system that helps people use complex digital services independently while staying in control of their personal data and decisions.

It focuses on improving access to public, healthcare, financial and other socially important services.

The problem

Digital services often assume that users can navigate complex interfaces, legal language and multi-step processes on their own. When difficulties arise, people turn to relatives, sometimes sharing passwords, confidential information and full control of their actions.

The problem hypothesis draws on recent UK digital-inclusion research, particularly Informal and Proxy Support for Digital Inclusion (DSIT, 2026), which examines how people receive help with digital services.

The research highlights more than gaps in digital skills: complex interfaces, fear of mistakes, uncertainty about consequences and reliance on other people also create barriers.

DSIT · Informal and proxy support for digital inclusion · 2026

The concept

Supported Access combines two mechanisms:

GUIDED

An adaptive mode that makes complex processes easier through step-by-step guidance, plain explanations and reduced cognitive load.

SUPPORT MODE

A secure collaboration mode for inviting a trusted person to explain, check or help with a specific action without granting full account access.

Help without taking over.

Work completed

  • Preliminary research into four global social problems and 16 product concepts.
  • Reviewed existing solutions, international digital-accessibility practices and unmet user needs.
  • Defined the product concept, target audience and 10 research hypotheses.
  • Developed a UX research strategy: participant segments, in-depth interview structure and recruitment principles.
  • Preliminary assessment of technical feasibility, privacy, security and legal constraints.
  • Outlined a possible MVP architecture and effectiveness metrics.

Project goals

Social

Make essential digital services more accessible, reduce dependency on others and protect autonomy and privacy.

Product

Develop and test a scalable adaptive-interaction system that existing digital services could potentially integrate.

Professional

Create a research-led Product / Service Design case for international competitions, including iF DESIGN STUDENT AWARD and D&AD Future Impact.

Next step

Conduct in-depth interviews, test the hypotheses, map the customer journey and build an interactive MVP for usability testing.

Case 01 · self-initiated concept

SELA — navigation & home screen

A UX audit and redesign concept for the mobile app: fewer unnecessary steps, clearer interactive elements, and faster access to products.

ChallengeSimplify navigation and product discovery
RoleUX/UI design
DeliverableConcept + prototype

Self-initiated portfolio concept. This is not an internal SELA project.

Before → after

Screen comparison

A comparison of the current app and the redesign concept: a new menu structure, more prominent search, and navigation patterns closer to familiar content-browsing behavior.

1 / 1

Problems

Three findings from the UX audit and the hypotheses that shaped the concept.

01

Unclear interactivity

Decorative and clickable banners look too similar, so it is not always clear which images lead to another screen.

Hypothesis

Use one consistent pattern: title, short description, and an explicit “View collection →” CTA.

02

Too many extra steps

To understand what some collections contain, users first have to open a separate screen.

Hypothesis

Preview products and looks in horizontal carousels directly on the home screen.

03

Overloaded menu

A large number of similar menu items makes scanning and finding the right category harder.

Hypothesis

Group nested categories and move collections into a more editorial home-screen structure.

Design decisions in detail

Solution

Navigation as a simple action matrix

01

Menu

Collection buttons are removed from the first level and moved into expandable lists. This reduces visual noise and makes the menu easier to scan.

02

Section structure

Core product categories — clothing, accessories, and footwear — are grouped visually, while “Coming soon” is moved lower as a planning scenario rather than an immediate purchase path.

03

Search

The search field is available directly on the home screen, shortening the path to a specific product.

04

Swipe patterns

Vertical and horizontal browsing follows patterns familiar from social products, reducing unnecessary taps.

05

Cards & banners

Product and look cards can be swiped within each section, quick-save is added, and banners get explicit CTAs so their interactivity is easier to recognize.

Validation plan

How I would validate the concept

Expected effects — not measured yet

Faster access to the catalog

Search and categories become more visible on the first screen.

Clearer menu hierarchy

The first level is shorter and supports orientation more effectively.

Fewer unnecessary steps

Collections and product cards can be explored directly from the home screen.

Higher navigation success

Users should find it easier to understand where to tap and how to continue browsing.

Primary metric Time on Task

Measure the time required to find a category, locate a product, and complete the flow through the menu and search.
