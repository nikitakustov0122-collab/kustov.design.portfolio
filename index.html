Case 01 · self-initiated concept

SELA — navigation & home screen

A UX audit and redesign concept for the mobile app: fewer unnecessary steps, clearer interactive elements, and faster access to products.

ChallengeSimplify navigation and product discovery
RoleUX/UI design
DeliverableConcept + prototype

Self-initiated portfolio concept. This is not an internal SELA project.

Before → after

Screen comparison

A comparison of the current app and the redesign concept: a new menu structure, more prominent search, and navigation patterns closer to familiar content-browsing behavior.

Problems

Three findings from the UX audit and the hypotheses that shaped the concept.

01

Unclear interactivity

Decorative and clickable banners look too similar, so it is not always clear which images lead to another screen.

Hypothesis

Use one consistent pattern: title, short description, and an explicit “View collection →” CTA.

02

Too many extra steps

To understand what some collections contain, users first have to open a separate screen.

Hypothesis

Preview products and looks in horizontal carousels directly on the home screen.

03

Overloaded menu

A large number of similar menu items makes scanning and finding the right category harder.

Hypothesis

Group nested categories and move collections into a more editorial home-screen structure.

Solution

Navigation as a simple action matrix

01

Menu

Collection buttons are removed from the first level and moved into expandable lists. This reduces visual noise and makes the menu easier to scan.

02

Section structure

Core product categories — clothing, accessories, and footwear — are grouped visually, while “Coming soon” is moved lower as a planning scenario rather than an immediate purchase path.

03

Search

The search field is available directly on the home screen, shortening the path to a specific product.

04

Swipe patterns

Vertical and horizontal browsing follows patterns familiar from social products, reducing unnecessary taps.

05

Cards & banners

Product and look cards can be swiped within each section, quick-save is added, and banners get explicit CTAs so their interactivity is easier to recognize.

Redesigned screens

The concept in detail

Seven screens show the new menu and home-screen logic, from top-level categories to nested sections and an editorial presentation of collections.

Case outcomes

Expected impact of the redesign

Faster access to the catalog

Search and categories become more visible on the first screen.

Clearer menu hierarchy

The first level is shorter and supports orientation more effectively.

Fewer unnecessary steps

Collections and product cards can be explored directly from the home screen.

Higher navigation success

Users should find it easier to understand where to tap and how to continue browsing.

Primary metric Time on Task

Measure the time required to find a category, locate a product, and complete the flow through the menu and search.

Prototype

Core flow: home → category → collection → product.

Case 02 · MVP in development

Polosa — music by independent artists

A service for discovering niche music, returning to artists, and directly purchasing music and merch.

RoleUX/UI + product design
ProcessResearch → prototype
ImplementationHTML/CSS + JavaScript
Polosa — independent music, stories, and direct artist support ↗
01

Niche music struggles to compete for attention

On major streaming platforms, users often remain inside familiar algorithmic feeds, while discovering smaller artists takes extra effort.

02

Artist support is fragmented across platforms

Listening, artist information, music purchases, and merch often live in separate services.

03

Information overload keeps growing

In a constant stream of content, it becomes harder for users to notice new artists they might genuinely like.

04

Payment can be difficult for part of the audience

For Russian users, purchases on international platforms often require additional steps.

Product goal

Shorten the journey from discovering an artist to saving them, returning, and choosing to support them.

Research

How people discover new music

A survey of active independent-music listeners in Saint Petersburg revealed recurring patterns in discovery, saving, and purchasing behavior.

01

No exact query

Discovery often starts with recommendations and browsing rather than a search field.

02

Music comes first

An artist’s first screen should let users hear the music as quickly as possible.

03

Saving is the core action

“Save” and “Follow” are the key actions after first discovery.

04

Return loops need to be designed

After following an artist, the product should create reasons to return: a release, concert, or artist update.

05

Purchase comes later

The store should not compete with the first listening experience. Interest comes first.

06

People buy value

Users buy a desirable object and a closer connection to the artist, not only the idea of providing support.

User jobs

“I want to discover new music even when I don’t know exactly what I’m looking for.”
“If an artist interests me, I want to save them quickly and follow their updates.”
“If I connect with an artist, I want an easy way to support them and get something meaningful in return.”

Journey map insights

Three critical moments

Discovery

“Who is this, and should I keep listening?”

Music, brief context, and current events are brought together in one place.

Return

“How do I avoid losing track of this artist?”

Following and updates help the relationship continue after the first interaction.

Purchase

“Why is this worth my money?”

The price is supported by product details, uniqueness, and context from the artist.

Visual system

Retro aesthetics as part of the positioning

Polosa is not only a service but also a media platform about niche artists. The visual system therefore draws from designer zines and early digital interfaces: simple, nostalgic, and less sterile than most contemporary music apps.

This approach helps the product stand out, supports its editorial character, and makes music discovery more memorable.

MVP structure

The home page as an entry point to both media and service

The long-form home prototype combines several scenarios: music discovery, quick access to the top chart, curated collections, editorial content, and the store.

  • the first screen centers on discovery and current music content;
  • editorial content and releases live in the same ecosystem;
  • the store appears as a next step after discovering an artist;
  • the long scroll presents the MVP as a connected media product rather than just a catalog.
